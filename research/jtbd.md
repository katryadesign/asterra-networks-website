**"When [situation], I want [motivation], so that [outcome]."**

[§7](#7-feature-check).

**Evidence labels** — same as the other files:
**[FACT]** primary source read · **[2ND-HAND]** from paraphrase · **[SURVEY]** ·

## 1. Short job stories — card format

Same content as §2–§4, at sticky-note length. For the board, the workshop and the deck.
Extended phrasings with the evidence base — from [§2](#2-main-job) onward.

### 🎯 Main

> **When** I need to close a fleet requirement, **I want** to understand what exactly we need and who can be trusted with it, **so that** I enter the conversation with an answer, not a question.

### Related

> **When** a new dated requirement comes out, **I want** to understand what it means for my vessels, **so that** I don't learn about non-compliance during an inspection.

> **When** I pick a supplier, **I want** to check that class and the auditor will accept their solution, **so that** my own choice doesn't compromise the company.

> **When** I already know what we need, **I want** to gather the arguments for those who hold the money, **so that** the decision doesn't stall in approval.

> **When** I find something that fits, **I want** to know at once whether we can buy it, **so that** I don't waste months on an option that gets blocked anyway.

> **When** the crew asks for decent internet, **I want** to grant access without losing control of the costs, **so that** people stay and the budget holds.

### Emotional

> **When** a topic goes beyond my specialty, **I want** to figure it out myself, **so that** I can talk to the expert as an equal.

> **When** I carry a decision upward, **I want** to be sure of my numbers, **so that** I don't look unserious.

### Social

> **When** we compete for crews, **I want** our vessels to be spoken of well, **so that** people join us and stay.

> **When** I report to management, **I want** to show the fleet is under control, **so that** I get asked next time too.

## 2. MAIN JOB

> ### 🎯 When I need to close a specific fleet requirement, I want to confidently determine what exactly we need and who can be trusted with it, so that I enter the supplier conversation with a ready position rather than the question "so what do you offer?"

**Personas:** P1 and P2 equally (P2 chooses, P1 pays, [`personas.md`](./personas.md) §10.1).

**Where it grew from:**
- The category's core pain, [`research.md`](./research.md) §9 — verbatim: the user cannot
  "(a) quickly grasp **which solution fits them**" and "(b) **trust the provider's reliability
  before any human contact**". **[AUDIT]**
- The product's job per the brief ([`../CLAUDE.md`](../CLAUDE.md) §1): let a professional buyer
  "quickly find the solution for their industry, understand its value, and reach out". **[FACT]**
- That this is genuinely the person's work rather than the site's is confirmed by P2's vacancy:
  "**Evaluate existing systems and make recommendations for future technology investments**"
  ([Mazu, ref. 1118549](https://www.mazurecruitment.com/jobs/it-manager-915339)). **[FACT]**
- That the market does not serve this work: of 15 sites none guides the user; Marlink solves it
  with a human — "Not sure where to start? **Talk to our team** and we'll point you in the
  right direction" ([`personas.md`](./personas.md) §10.5). **[FACT]**

**The progress:** from "there's a requirement and I don't know its scope" → to "I know what we
need, and I have a candidate I'm not embarrassed to name out loud".

## 3. RELATED JOBS — on the way to main

### R1. Translate a norm into a requirement for one's own vessels

> **When a dated requirement appears — a port inspection, the annual audit, a newbuild rule —
> I want to understand what exactly it means for my vessels, so that I don't discover
> non-compliance at inspection time.**

**Personas:** P1, P2.
**Evidence:**
- MLC 2006 amendments in force **23.12.2024**: "Shipowners… provide seafarers on board their ships with **internet access with charges, if any, being reasonable in amount**" ([ILO](https://www.ilo.org/resource/news/new-important-set-amendments-mlc-2006-will-enter-force-23-december-2024)) · enforced via **Port State Control** ([Chambers](https://chambers.com/articles/mlc-2006-amendments-new-seafarer-rights-and-psc-compliance-obligations)). **[FACT]**
- MSC.428(98): cyber risks in the SMS by the first annual DOC verification after 01.01.2021 → a recurring deadline ([IMO](https://www.imo.org/en/ourwork/security/pages/cyber-security.aspx)). **[2ND-HAND]**
- IACS UR E26/E27 — only ships contracted from 01.07.2024 ([ClassNK](https://www.classnk.or.jp/hp/en/activities/cybersecurity/ur-e26e27.html)). **[2ND-HAND]**
- That the comprehension gap is real: an industry survey records that only 1 in 6 shipowners understands what a cyber-secure vessel should look like ([HFW/CyberOwl/Thetius](https://www.hfw.com/about-us/news/hfw-cyberowl-highlight-urgency-maritime-industry-tackling-growing-cyber-threats/), 150+ stakeholders). **[SURVEY + 2ND-HAND]**
- P1 documentedly monitors "vessels **Class and trading certificates**" and holds "knowledge… covered by **ISM**" ([seacareer](https://www.seacareer.com/i31473/technical-superintendent/)). **[FACT]**

⚠️ **What is hypothesis here:** that people bring this question **to a supplier** rather than
to the classification society, the P&I club or the flag administration — **[?]**.

### R2. Make sure the choice survives external scrutiny

> **When I consider a supplier, I want to make sure their solution will survive the scrutiny of
> class, the auditor or the inspector, so that my own choice doesn't compromise the company.**

**Personas:** P1 (primary), P2.
**Evidence:**
- P1's duty — "monitor vessels **Class and trading certificates** with the Marine Department" **[FACT]**
- The category answers with the body's name: Navarino — "UR E27 Type Approved" (**Bureau Veritas**), "IEC 62443-3-3 Type Approved" (**DNV**), IMO2021 ([screen](./screens/hard/navarino-cybersecurity-angel.png)) **[AUDIT]**
- Marlink put it on the homepage as a chip row: `ISO 9001 · ISO 14001 · ISO 27001 · IACS UR E26/E27 · NIS 2 · DORA` (verified 05.08.2026, [`personas.md`](./personas.md) §10.3) **[FACT]**
- The flip side: "only **55% of industry suppliers are asked by shipowners to prove** they have cyber risk management procedures" ([CyberOwl/HFW](https://cyberowl.io/cyberowl-hfw-report-maritime-industry-pays-average-3m-ransom-in-cyberattacks/)) **[SURVEY + 2ND-HAND]** — i.e. nearly half do **not** perform this job. The job exists, but not in everyone.

### R3. Assemble a rationale that survives the person with the budget

> **When I already know what we need, I want to assemble a rationale that convinces whoever
> controls the money, so that the decision doesn't stall in approval.**

**Personas:** P2 prepares → P4 receives.
**Evidence — the best-documented job in the file:**
- P2's vacancy verbatim: "**Prepare business cases and recommendations** for technology improvements, **balancing operational needs with commercial considerations**" **[FACT]**
- Its requirements: "**Commercial mindset with the ability to justify investments** and recommend practical solutions" **[FACT]**
- To whom it must be proven: the owner holds "Long-term asset strategy, **Major capital expenditure**", the manager runs OPEX within "an **approval matrix**" ([AVS](https://www.avsglobalsupply.com/blog-detail/ship-owner-ship-manager-procurement-decision)) **[FACT]**
- Why the pressure is price-shaped: "Management contracts are negotiated **mainly on price**, margins… are squeezed" ([Valour](https://valourconsultancy.com/loose-specs-sink-shipmanagers-maritime-ecosystem/)) **[FACT]**
- The budget frame for the cyber part: more than half of owners spend <US$100k/yr ([CyberOwl/HFW](https://cyberowl.io/cyberowl-hfw-report-maritime-industry-pays-average-3m-ransom-in-cyberattacks/)) **[SURVEY + 2ND-HAND]**

### R5. Give the crew what it demands without losing control

> **When the crew demands decent internet and I answer for costs and order on board, I want to
> find the balance between access and control, so that I retain people without losing either
> the budget or the manageability.**

**Personas:** P1 (invoices, the vessel), P4 (fleet-wide decision).
**Evidence — the only job with a direct buyer quote:**
- Patrick O'Halloran, COO, Overseas Shipholding Group, on the fleet-wide installation: "**Our crew identified this as important, and we listened**" ([BusinessWire, 19.12.2023](https://markets.financialcontent.com/ms.intelvalue/article/bizwire-2023-12-19-overseas-shipholding-group-installs-starlink-satellite-internet-service-on-entire-fleet)) **[FACT]**
- Both halves of the balance from one source, Geneva Dry 2025, Audra Drablos (Inmarsat): "The **first question potential crew ask is whether there's internet on board** – before taking the job" **and** owners request "**time-restricted access – perhaps a couple of hours daily – to help manage fatigue**"; "camaraderie… is diminishing as crew retreat to cabins" ([Riviera](https://www.rivieramm.com/news-content-hub/13-of-vessels-operate-without-crew-internet-highlighting-maritime-connectivity-gap-84738)) **[FACT]**
- The legal half: MLC 2006 + PSC (see R1) **[FACT]**
- The unserved market's size: "**13% of vessels** operate without crew internet" (same source) **[FACT]**

**Why this job deserves the spotlight:** it is the only one backed by a direct buyer quote, a
norm, and a live from-the-deck reality (§9.2) at once.

## 7. Feature check

Every phrasing run through the rule: no feature name may appear in "I want".

**What I caught in my own drafts while writing:**

| Caught | Why it's a feature | Became |
|---|---|---|
| "…I want to go through a solution picker" | "solution picker" = the Solution Finder | "…I want to confidently determine what we need" |
| "…I want to see certificates on the page" | "certificates on the page" = the proof block | "…I want to make sure the choice survives external scrutiny" |
| "…I want a comparison table" | a component name | moved to [§6 Hypotheses](#6-hypotheses) — no demand recorded |
| "…I want to download the specification" | an artifact name | dissolved into R3: "assemble a rationale that convinces…" |
| "…I want to write to an expert via the form" | channel + component | in the main job: "enter the conversation with a ready position" |

**A control pass over the final phrasings:** no "I want" contains the words
*finder, filter, form, table, calculator, page, catalog, chat, PDF, landing, button*.
Each holds a progress verb: *determine · understand · make sure · assemble · find the balance ·
figure out · come across*.

### 8.2 🎯 The MVP core — three jobs

Criterion: **matters to the primary (P1 = 3)** × **the market doesn't serve it**.

Three qualify:

**1. MAIN — "what exactly do we need and who to entrust it to"**
P1 = 3, P2 = 3. Market: the only finder was IEC's and, per the 05.08.2026 check, it vanished
from the menu; Marlink covers this **with a human**. It is the product's declared job per the
brief and the category's core pain per `research.md` §9. **Without it the site has no point.**

**2. R1 — "what does this norm mean for my vessels"** *(narrowed 05.08 after verification)*
P1 = 3, P2 = 3. Vendors offer no norm-based entry (checked across all five). **But** explaining
the norm is classification-society territory: ClassNK maintains a full compliance
infrastructure (guidelines, 137 Q&As, document samples). Competing with class on "understand
the norm" is a losing game. **The precise shape of the job for us: "norm → which solution
closes it → what paper you get."** Nobody owns that link — class doesn't sell solutions,
vendors don't lead from the norm. The entry has a date and external enforcement, and needs no
invented numbers — critical for a fictional brand.

**3. R5 — "give the crew access without losing control"** *(refined 05.08)*
P1 = 3, P4 = 3. At product level this is already sold (Navarino Infinity: "Crew Internet access
control during shifts"); **at message level nobody leads with it**. The only job backed by a
direct buyer quote, a legal norm, and — after the Reddit check (§9) — **twenty live accounts
from the deck**. Our opportunity is **the altitude of the message**, not inventing the feature.

**Deliberately not in the core:**
**R2** — P1 = 3, but the market serves it well (Navarino, Marlink). **Table stakes**: can't
skip it, can't win with it. **R3** — strong for P2/P4, but only 2 for the primary; it settles

## 9. Live voices — a forum and review check (05.08.2026)

### 9.1 The category's headline asymmetry

Reddit, Trustpilot and Glassdoor checked for the direct competitors and one reference company
from our segment (Cyprus/Greece, maritime satcom + digital).

**A public layer of customer reviews does not exist in this category.** Searching Marlink,
Navarino, Speedcast, NSSLGlobal on Trustpilot and Reddit yields **zero** customer reviews — the
results drown in unrelated noise. The reference company has **8 Glassdoor reviews, rated 4.2,
all from employees**, none from customers. **[FACT]**

> ### ⭐ An asymmetry worth carrying into the work
> **The end user (the crew) is loud online. The buyer (superintendent / fleet IT) is silent.**
> Seafarers discuss connectivity publicly and in detail; the people who purchase it leave no
> trace anywhere.
>
> This explains why the whole "buyer behaviour" block in our files sits at **[?]**, and why
> that is **a property of the market, not a search failure**. It also points to where the voice
> does exist: not in supplier reviews, but **in crew forums** — where the **consequences** of
> buyers' decisions are visible.

### 9.2 What the crew forum shows — and why it confirms R5

The r/maritime thread "What internet do you use onboard…" (June 2026, 35 comments,
≈20 distinct vessels described) — [link](https://www.reddit.com/r/maritime/comments/1u6p9qq/what_internet_do_you_use_onboard_how_much_does_it/). **[FACT — thread read]**

**The key observation: nearly every vessel describes not the link, but the policy.**

| What seafarers describe | Examples from the thread (paraphrase) |
|---|---|
| **A daily or monthly quota** | from 450 MB/day to 1.5 GB/day; 12 GB/month for crew; 80 GB/month per person; a vessel cap of 1–2 TB/month |
| **Rank-based tariffs** | crew 12 GB vs senior officers 40 GB; unlimited only on the bridge and in the ECR; the "top 4" uncapped |
| **Content filtering** | streaming blocked, some site categories unreachable, a mandatory firewall |
| **Deliberate speed throttling** | so the monthly cap doesn't burn out in the first days |
| **Per-user management** | a captive portal distributing data so it lasts the voyage |
| **Geographic rules** | switched off in port and in Chinese waters |
| **Backup and hybrid** | Starlink + Inmarsat as fallback; Starlink + Viasat; migration to OneWeb; coverage gaps |
| **Paid top-ups** | $2.50/GB called expensive; elsewhere 15 GB for $15; elsewhere 1 GB for $25 and "very slow" |

One short line carrying the end user's mood:

> "You have internet?" — a comment with 11 upvotes

**What this changes:**

1. **R5 is confirmed from the second side.** The evidence used to come only from the buyer's
   side (Inmarsat on "time-restricted access", the COO quote). Now the **implementation** of
   those decisions is visible on ~20 vessels. R5 moves from "backed by two sources" to **the
   best-evidenced job in the file**.
2. **The product being bought is a rule set, not bandwidth.** Quotas, ranks, filters,
   geography, per-user distribution, a backup channel. At message level nobody speaks this
   language; at product level it already exists — Navarino Infinity sells "Crew Internet access
   control during shifts" and QoS as platform features (correction 05.08, see §3 R5).
3. **Redundancy is a separate, previously unnoticed pattern.** Vessels keep **two channels**
   (Starlink + Inmarsat/Viasat/OneWeb). This maps directly onto Asterra's positioning in
   [`../CLAUDE.md`](../CLAUDE.md) §3 — "one integrated provider… where downtime is not an option".
4. **Top-up pricing is a live crew pain point.** Tariff transparency can be an argument for P1,
   who signs those invoices.

⚠️ **This source's limits.** This is **the crew**, not the buyer. The thread shows **what**
buyers decided, not **why**, nor **how they chose the supplier**. Commenter self-selection is
uncontrolled. Use as evidence of consequences, not as the target audience's voice.

## 10. What follows for the design

One line per job — no feature names, so the job doesn't get swapped for a solution.

1. **MAIN** — the site must bring the person to "I know what we need", not to "I saw your catalog". Those are different end states.
2. **R1** — a dated norm is the strongest conversation entry, and it is free in the category: everyone shows certificates as badges, nobody lets you enter through them ([`personas.md`](./personas.md) §10.3).
3. **R2** — proof must name **who confirmed it** (DNV, Bureau Veritas), not just the fact of compliance. The body's name outweighs the number here.
4. **R3** — everything the person sees, they will have to retell upward. So every claim must be **memorably reproducible** and self-consistent — this is exactly where number drift costs the most.
5. **R4** — the question "can you even buy this?" is better asked early and by us than discovered as a frame agreement at the third meeting.
6. **R5** — speak of **manageability**, not just speed. A confirmed need no one owns.
7. **E1** — language decides whether the person stays at all: class, certificate, ISM, dock, budget — yes; OSI layers and COTP/COTM — no.
8. **S1** — the crew as an argument works at leadership level, proven by a direct COO quote. Not a "welfare section" — a staff-retention argument.
