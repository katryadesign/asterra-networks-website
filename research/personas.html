Section 02

Jobs hierarchy

Format: "When [situation], I want [motivation], so that [outcome]". No "I want" names a feature — only human progress. The jobs describe progress in work documented by duties and regulations; that it happens on a supplier's website is the project's bet [?].

🎯 Main job

When I need to close a specific fleet requirement, I want to confidently determine what we need and who to entrust it to, so that I enter the supplier conversation with a position rather than with "so what do you offer?"

P1 + P2 (P2 chooses, P1 pays) · Evidence: the category pain "which solution fits them", from an audit of 15 competitor sites AUDIT § · "Evaluate existing systems…" in P2's vacancy FACT ↗ · Marlink closes this with a human: "Not sure where to start? Talk to our team" FACT §

Related — on the way to the main job

R1 · narrowed after the ClassNK check

When a requirement with a date appears, I want to understand what it means for my vessels, so that I don't learn about non-compliance at the moment of inspection.

R2

When I'm considering a supplier, I want to be sure their solution will survive scrutiny from class, an auditor or an inspector, so that I don't expose the company through my own choice.

P1 (primarily), P2 · The duty to "monitor vessels Class and trading certificates" FACT ↗ · "Validation" is a universal B2B job per Gartner FACT ↗ · But: only 55% of suppliers are ever asked to prove cyber procedures — so 45% of buyers don't perform this job SURVEY2ND-HAND ↗

R3 · best documented

When I already know what we need, I want to assemble a rationale that will convince whoever controls the money, so that the decision doesn't stall in approval.

R4

When I find a suitable solution, I want to understand early whether we can even buy it under our existing agreements, so that I don't spend months on an option that gets blocked anyway.

P4, P2 · "Supplier change may require approval when a frame agreement already exists" FACT ↗ · Trace in the category: Reseller / Partnership request types in Marlink's form AUDIT ▦

R5 · best supported overall — three independent paths

When the crew demands proper internet and I'm answerable for cost and order on board, I want to find the balance between access and control, so that I keep the people without losing either the budget or manageability.

Section 04

MVP core and cut candidates

Core criterion: important to the primary (P1 = 3) × not served by the market. The matrix answers two questions: what to do first and what not to do at all.

1 · MAIN

"What exactly we need and who to entrust it to." A finder existed only at IEC — and vanished from the menu by the 05.08 re-check §; Marlink closes this with a human §. Without this job the site has no point.

2 · R1 narrowed

Not "explain the regulation" (that's class-society territory — ClassNK keeps 137 Q&As, guidelines and document samples ↗), but the link "regulation → which solution closes it → what paper you get". The only entry point with a date and external enforcement; requires no invented numbers. Risk: whether a deadline leads to the site — [?] not confirmed

3 · R5 strongest

Access vs control. Three independent evidence paths: the COO quote ↗, trade press since 2017 ↗, ≈20 live accounts from on board ↗. Sold in the depths (Navarino Infinity) ↗ — what's unclaimed is the altitude of the message. The only core element with no dangerous hypothesis underneath it.

Deliberately not in the core

R2 — table stakes

P1 = 3, but the market covers it well (Navarino type approval, Marlink chips). You can't skip it, and you can't win with it.

R3 — a format, not a work block

Strong for P2/P4, a 2 for the primary. It lands on the core as the way every solution page is presented ("forwardability").

The biggest hypothesis — underneath the whole document

That this work is done on a supplier's website at all. No public layer of buyer feedback exists in this category — Trustpilot and Reddit searched for Marlink, Navarino, Speedcast and NSSLGlobal returned zero customer reviews; a reference company has 8 Glassdoor reviews, all from employees §. The end user is loud online, the buyer is silent. That is a property of the market, not a failure of searching. It is settled by one question to sales: who actually sends inbound enquiries, and after what.
