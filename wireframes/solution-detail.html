Asterra Shield

Cyber protection for fleets, specified against the framework your auditor names, with the evidence prepared before the verification rather than after it.

Talk to an expert See the regulations it closes

The audit is where cyber fails first.

An attack on a vessel is survivable; most are contained. What sinks budgets and reputations is the annual Document of Compliance verification, when the auditor asks where cyber risk lives in the safety management system and the honest answer is nowhere yet. Newbuilds contracted from 1 July 2024 arrive with IACS UR E26/E27 on top.

Shield is the protection and the evidence in one specification: what runs on the network, what runs on devices, and what the auditor reads.

A situation brings a fleet here.

  • The DOC verification is booked Cyber risk has to be readable in the SMS before that date
  • A newbuild is contracted UR E26/E27 applies to ships contracted from 1 July 2024
  • The fleet is being standardised One specification across vessels, not per-vessel improvisation
  • The cyber budget has to stretch One decision, sized against everything else the budget carries
  • Port State Control named cyber in a finding Findings close with evidence, not assurances

Rewritten in the review pass — headings now carry claims

The first version was headed by taxonomy labels ("Situations it answers", "Budget-grade facts", "Where it applies"): the IA showing through the page. Every section now leads with the claim its content makes ("The audit is where cyber fails first", "What it closes, and the paper you keep", "The facts for the budget file"), situations read as sentences, and the sector cross-link folded into the closing decision block instead of holding a section of its own. Same objects (O1 fields, O2 links, O5 attributes), same flow exits, different register. The head gained the brief §6 contextual inquiry CTA, which is mandated for solution pages.
